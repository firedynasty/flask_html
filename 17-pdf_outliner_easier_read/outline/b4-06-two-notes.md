# Two Notes

- In order to avoid misunderstanding I here add notes on two points arising out of the last chapter.
- (1) One sensible critic wrote asking me why, if God wanted sons instead of ‘toy soldiers’, He did not beget many sons at the outset instead of first making toy soldiers and then bringing them to life by such a difficult and painful process
  - The process of being turned from a creature into a son would not have been difficult or painful if the human race had not turned away from God centuries ago [restatement]
    - They were able to do this because He gave them free will: He gave them free will because a world of mere automata could never love and therefore never know infinite happiness [restatement]
      - The difficult part is this [restatement]
  - If we insist on asking ‘But could there have been many?’ we find ourselves in very deep water [qualification]
  - But when you are talking about God—i.e. about the rock bottom, irreducible Fact on which all other facts depend—it is nonsensical to ask if it could have been otherwise [contrast]
    - It is what it is, and there is an end of the matter [restatement]
  - But quite apart from this, I find a difficulty about the very idea of the Father begetting many sons from all eternity [contrast]
    - In order to be many they would have to be somehow different from one another [restatement]
  - In other words, to think of them as different, we have had to bring in space and matter; in fact we have had to bring in ‘Nature’ or the created universe [restatement]
    - I can understand the distinction between the Father and the Son without bringing in space or matter, because the one begets and the other is begotten [restatement]
      - The Father’s relation to the Son is not the same as the Son’s relation to the Father [restatement]
  - But if there were several sons they would all be related to one another and to the Father in the same way [contrast]
  - One does not notice the difficulty at first, of course [restatement]
    - One thinks one can form the idea of several ‘sons’ [restatement]
  - But when I think closely, I find that the idea seemed possible only because I was vaguely imagining them as human forms standing about together in some kind of space [contrast]
  - In other words, though I pretended to be thinking about something that exists before any universe was made, I was really smuggling in the picture of a universe and putting that something inside it [restatement]
  - When I stop doing that and still try to think of the Father begetting many sons ‘before all worlds’ I find I am not really thinking of anything [qualification]
  - (Was Nature—space and time and matter—created precisely in order to make many-ness possible [restatement]
    - Is there perhaps no other way of getting many eternal spirits except by first making many natural creatures, in a universe, and then spiritualising them [restatement]
  - But of course all this is guesswork.) [contrast]
- One part of the answer to this question is fairly easy: the other part is probably beyond all human knowledge
- The easy part is this
- All Christians are agreed that there is, in the full and original sense, only one ‘Son of God’
- Have the words ‘Could have been’ any sense at all when applied to God
- You can say that one particular finite thing ‘could have been’ different from what it is, because it would have been different if something else had been different, and the something else would have been different if some third thing had been different, and so on
- (The letters on this page would have been red if the printer had used red ink, and he would have used red ink if he had been instructed to, and so on
- Two pennies have the same shape
- How are they two
- By occupying different places and containing different atoms
- How would they differ from one another
- The idea fades away into mere words
- (2) The idea that the whole human race is, in a sense, one thing—one huge organism, like a tree—must not be confused with the idea that individual differences do not matter or that real people, Tom and Nobby and Kate, are somehow less important than collective things like classes, races, and so forth
  - Indeed the two ideas are opposites [restatement]
    - Things which are parts of a single organism may be very different from one another: things which are not, may be very alike [restatement]
      - Six pennies are quite separate and very alike: my nose and my lungs are very different but they are only alive at all because they are parts of my body and share its common life [restatement]
  - Christianity thinks of human individuals not as mere members of a group or items in a list, but as organs in a body—different from one another and each contributing what no other could [restatement]
  - When you find yourself wanting to turn your children, or pupils, or even your neighbours, into people exactly like yourself, remember that God probably never meant them to be that [qualification]
  - On the other hand, when you are tempted not to bother about someone else’s troubles because they are ‘no business of yours’, remember that though he is different from you he is part of the same organism as you [contrast]
  - If you forget that he belongs to the same organism as yourself you will become an Individualist [qualification]
  - If you forget that he is a different organ from you, if you want to suppress differences and make people all alike, you will become a Totalitarian [qualification]
  - But a Christian must not be either a Totalitarian or an Individualist. [contrast]
- You and they are different organs, intended to do different things
- I feel a strong desire to tell you—and I expect you feel a strong desire to tell me—which of these two errors is the worse
  - That is the devil getting at us [restatement]
    - He always sends errors into the world in pairs—pairs of opposites [restatement]
      - And he always encourages us to spend a lot of time thinking which is the worse [restatement]
  - He relies on your extra dislike of the one error to draw you gradually into the opposite one [restatement]
  - But do not let us be fooled [contrast]
    - We have to keep our eyes on the goal and go straight through between both errors [restatement]
- You see why, of course
- We have no other concern than that with either of them.
